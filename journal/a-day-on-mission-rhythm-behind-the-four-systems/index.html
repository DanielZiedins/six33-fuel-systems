A Day on Mission: The Rhythm Behind the Four Systems

05:00 rise. 09:00 deep work. 17:00 after effort. 21:00 wind down. The daily rhythm behind SIX33's four systems — and the spiritual practices that belong at each point.

Daniel ZiedinsFounder, SIX33 Fuel Systems

SIX33 has four systems because a day has four turning points. We call it the Fuel Map: rise, deep work, after effort, wind down. The times are a guide, not a rule — a nurse on nights and a parent of a newborn will draw it differently. But the shape holds for almost everyone.

This post isn't about what goes in a packet. It's about what a day on mission can look like, and the practices that have shaped Christians for centuries at each of those four points.

05:00 — Rise

Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up, left the house and went off to a solitary place, where he prayed.

Mark 1:35 (NIV)

Jesus had a crowded life: people pressing in, needs everywhere. His response wasn't to start earlier on the work. It was to start earlier with His Father.

The first hour sets the posture of the day. A few ideas:

  • Move before you scroll. Get up, get light, get moving — before the phone gets a vote.
  • Read before you react. A short passage of scripture before anyone else's words.
  • Pray the calendar. Look at the day ahead and hand each appointment to God by name.

09:00 — Deep work

The middle of the morning is when most of us have the clearest heads. It's worth protecting.

Deep work is a spiritual discipline as much as a productivity one. Doing a task with your full attention — without splitting it across tabs and notifications — is a way of honouring both the work and the One you're doing it for.

  • Decide the one thing. What single piece of work, done well, would make today count?
  • Close the doors. Notifications off, door shut, one window open.
  • Offer it. Colossians 3:23 is a good thing to say quietly before you begin.

If planning is where your days fall apart, a simple tool helps. TaskSimply was built for exactly this kind of work-family-ministry juggling.

17:00 — After effort

The end of the working day is where many people quietly fall apart — not from the work itself, but from carrying it home.

This is a good moment for two things: physical recovery after whatever you poured out, and an honest debrief with God. What went well? Where did I fall short? Who do I need to make things right with?

Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.

Matthew 11:28 (NIV)

The transition home matters too. A short walk, a change of clothes, five minutes in the car before you go inside — small markers that tell your mind the workday is over and the people in front of you get the best of you.

21:00 — Wind down

Rest is where most modern rhythms break. We stay up late, scroll in bed, and wonder why the next 05:00 feels impossible.

In vain you rise early and stay up late, toiling for food to eat — for he grants sleep to those he loves.

Psalm 127:2 (NIV)

Going to bed on time is an act of trust. It says: the world will keep turning without me for eight hours, because it was never mine to hold up.

  • Set a screens-off time and keep the phone out of reach.
  • Examine the day. A short look back — thanks for what was good, confession for what wasn't.
  • End with a psalm. Psalm 4 and Psalm 91 were written for exactly this hour.

Build it slowly

Nobody installs a whole rhythm overnight. Pick one of these four points and get it right for a fortnight before you touch another. If you'd like a tool that helps you shape your days around what matters, Reborn: Life is free and built for it.

SIX33's four systems are designed to sit inside this rhythm — a small, physical cue at each turning point. But the rhythm comes first. The fuel only serves it.

This is general encouragement, not medical advice. Talk to a doctor about sleep, training or nutrition that's specific to you.
