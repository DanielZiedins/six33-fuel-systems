Seek First: What Matthew 6:33 Means on an Ordinary Tuesday

Matthew 6:33 is the verse SIX33 is named after. Here's what “seek first” looks like when the day is ordinary, the inbox is full and nobody is watching.

Daniel ZiedinsFounder, SIX33 Fuel Systems

Every SIX33 packet carries a number before it carries a name. Six. Thirty-three. It points to one sentence Jesus spoke on a hillside to people who were worried about ordinary things — food, clothes, tomorrow.

But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.

Matthew 6:33 (NIV)

It's one of the most quoted verses in the Bible, which makes it easy to admire and easy to skip. So here's the question we kept asking while building SIX33: what does seek first look like on an ordinary Tuesday?

It's an order, not an add-on

Notice what Jesus doesn't say. He doesn't say "seek the kingdom too," or "make time for God when things calm down." He says first. The verse is about sequence. Before the plan, before the pressure, before the scroll — the King.

That's uncomfortable, because most of us already have a first. It might be work. It might be the approval of the people we're trying to impress. It might simply be whatever buzzes loudest. Seeking first means deciding, on purpose, what gets the opening minutes of the day and the final word on our decisions.

What it looks like before 9AM

For most people the battle for "first" is won or lost early. A few practical shapes it can take:

  • The first words. Before you read anyone else's words — messages, headlines, notifications — read a few of His. One psalm. One chapter. One verse, if that's honest.
  • The first conversation. Talk to God before you talk to anyone else. It doesn't need to be eloquent. "Here's my day. It's yours" is a real prayer.
  • The first decision. Name one thing today where you'll choose what's right over what's easy. Write it down if you have to.

None of this is a formula, and none of it earns anything. It's simply putting the King where He belongs — at the front.

What it looks like at 2PM

Seeking first isn't only a morning habit. It shows up in the middle of the day, when the real tests come: the shortcut nobody would notice, the colleague who's easy to ignore, the moment you could take credit that isn't yours.

Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.

Colossians 3:23 (NIV)

That's the verse printed on every Flow packet, and it reframes the whole afternoon. The spreadsheet, the training session, the dishes — all of it becomes worship when it's done for Him. Seeking first doesn't take you out of ordinary work. It changes who the work is for.

"All these things"

The second half of the verse is a promise, and it's easy to misread. It isn't a guarantee that following Jesus makes life easy or profitable. The "things" Jesus has just been talking about are food and clothing — the basics people lie awake over. His point is that a Father who feeds birds and dresses fields knows what His children need.

The promise frees you from carrying tomorrow on your own back. You can work hard without being ruled by anxiety, because the outcome was never yours to hold.

Why we put it on the box

We named SIX33 after this verse because we wanted something you'd see every day — on the counter, in the gym bag, at the desk — that pointed past itself. A stick pack can't make you seek first. But it can be a small, daily reminder of the decision, and maybe start a conversation with someone who asks what the number means.

If you've never really thought about what following Jesus involves, I AM REBORN is a good, honest place to start. And if you want to go deeper with the movement behind SIX33, that's Seek First World.

Seek first. Then go to work.
